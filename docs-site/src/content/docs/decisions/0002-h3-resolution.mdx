## Context

H3 hexagons join rasters, polygons and the graph, so graph queries never do
heavy geometry ([technical requirements §3](../../research/technical-requirements/)).
The requirements say "resolution ~8–9". Most of our users' borrowers are
family farms, which by law (Lei 11.326) hold at most four fiscal modules, and
many hold a few tens of hectares or less. The resolution decides whether a
small farm gets its own cells or is blurred into its neighbours.

## Try it

Pick a parcel size and move the resolution. The cells are computed in your
browser with [h3-js](https://github.com/uber/h3-js).

## Options

Cell counts for the synthetic parcels in the explorer above:

| Resolution | Average cell | Small plot (6 ha) | Family farm (25 ha) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | ~74 ha | 0 (centroid cell) | 1 | One cell spans several family farms |
| **9** | **~10.5 ha** | **1** | **3** | Each farm gets its own cells |
| 10 | ~1.5 ha | 5 | 18 | About 6–7× the rows of resolution 9 |

## Decision

Index parcels at **resolution 9**. Hazard rasters (30 m to 10 km) are
aggregated to resolution 8 parents with `cell_to_parent`, so a parcel joins
its hazard through the H3 hierarchy without a geometric overlay. Exact
overlap areas (CAR × indigenous land and the like) stay in PostGIS, in an
equal-area projection.

## Consequences

- Good: small farms keep their own cells, and the graph stays at roughly one
  to a few dozen cells per property.
- Bad: parcels under ~10 ha can have no cell centre inside them, so they fall
  back to the centroid cell. Flags for such parcels must come from PostGIS
  overlaps, not from cell matches.
- Revisit if per-plot insurance claims need sub-hectare precision.
